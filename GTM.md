# Go-To-Market

## Exact target user

**Primary:** Engineering Manager or CTO at a Series A/B SaaS company, 10–50 employees, at least 5 engineers. They own the tooling budget, they receive the monthly Stripe invoice for Cursor/Copilot/Claude, and they've thought "this feels expensive" at least once — but they haven't done anything about it because there's no obvious action to take. They are not a finance person; they're a technical person who has inherited budget responsibility.

**Secondary:** Founder/CEO at a 3–10 person dev-tool startup who directly pays for AI subscriptions. They are even more price-sensitive (every dollar is personal), even less likely to have benchmarks, and disproportionately active on X and Hacker News.

## How to get the first 100 users in 30 days with $0 budget

**Week 1 — Warm network + targeted HN post**

Post on Hacker News as "Show HN: SpendLens — free AI tool spend auditor (no signup)." The HN audience is exactly the target user (eng managers, founders). The free + no-signup combination is specifically designed for this audience who have high "don't waste my time" filters. A genuine Show HN with real savings examples has historically driven 200–500 users in 24 hours.

DM 20 founders/CTOs directly with a personalized message: "Hey [name] — I built something that might be relevant to you. Takes 2 min, no signup. Curious what your numbers look like." Do not mass-send; write individually.

**Week 2 — Twitter/X thread with real example output**

Post a thread: "I audited our AI tool spend and found $340/mo in savings we didn't know about. Here's what we were overpaying for: [screenshot of SpendLens results]." The shareable URL feature is specifically designed for this — the screenshot includes the savings number, which is inherently shareable.

Tag @swyx, @levelsio, @karpathy — they actively engage with dev tools and have audiences of exactly the right people.

**Week 3 — Subreddit posts with example audits**

Post example audits (with fictional but realistic numbers) on r/ExperiencedDevs, r/SaaS, r/devops. "Ran our AI spend through a free tool — found we were paying for both Cursor Pro and GitHub Copilot for the same 8 engineers. $152/mo we didn't need to spend." This is a value-first post, not a product plug.

**Week 4 — LinkedIn posts from founder angle**

Post about the tool from a "I built this, here's what I learned" framing. LinkedIn's algo favors this format. Founders and ops people (the secondary audience) are more active on LinkedIn than Twitter.
